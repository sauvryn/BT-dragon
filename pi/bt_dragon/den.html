Care and Accessibility

Sleep screen
Like an e-reader's: during a nap or sleep, Harald's screen shows this instead of whatever it showed last (e-ink keeps its picture even with the power off). Use your own pictures under Artwork.
Color Blindness Mode
Colors for each kind of color blindness, on every page of the Den.
Den password (In Development, Untested)
Asks for a password before the Den opens, for when the Den can be reached from outside your own network (see Tailscale in the README). Each phone or computer logs in once and stays logged in for 30 days. The phone voice has its own ticket: download its script again after setting or changing the password.

Backup

A small file with everything that's yours: Harald, the diary, lands, the shopping list, calendar, voice profiles, your artwork, your settings and Developer Tools knobs. It includes your hotspot passwords and WiGLE token, so keep it somewhere private.

Without the den: copy a backup onto the card's boot partition (the one Windows can open), named bt-dragon-restore.tar.gz (the older name, harald-restore.tar.gz, works too), and Harald restores it on the next start.

The moon

On the day of each new moon, first quarter, full moon and last quarter, Harald gets a burst of joy (the full moon is the biggest) and announces it: on the screen, and out loud if your dragon has a voice. No internet needed: the phases are worked out on the Pi.

Butterfly Garden

Insects raised on spare food

Raise insects from Harald's spare food: snacks and feasts that don't fit in the BELLY, and treats and berries that fit in neither the BELLY nor the PACK, go to the larvae here, shared evenly between them. Once a larva pupates it eats nothing more: the adult emerges by itself 24 hours later. Data imported from another of the project author's repos, Bookworm, using its data of species, field notes, and pupation timings. Manual fact-checking of Bookworm's data is ongoing.

Spiders aren't included at the moment. If they're ever added, there will be an "arachnophobia" tick box here to keep them from hatching.

Opens when Harald reaches level 5.

Ask Harald

Type a question and the answer appears below (and is said out loud, if your dragon has a voice). Try: the time in Tokyo, the date, "set a pasta timer for twelve minutes", what's on the shopping list or the calendar, the weather, or at home: "what's the temperature at home?", the humidity, air pressure, pollen count, UV index, air quality, sunrise or sunset.

Harald only knows a set range of questions (the full list is in the README), and the answers don't come from a search engine. For anything else, use a real search engine (DuckDuckGo, for example) or an assistant that uses one (Hey Google, Siri, Alexa and so on).

RSS feeds · news from websites you choose

Many websites publish a feed (RSS or Atom): a list of their newest articles, episodes or posts. Paste a feed's address and press Add, and Harald checks it on the clock: every 20 minutes to start with (6:00, 6:20, 6:40...), or as often as you set for each feed below (counted from midnight, so 30 means on the hour and half past). Anything new is on the e-ink screen (RSS, the source and as much of the title as fits) and said out loud if your dragon has a voice ("New RSS for BBC News", then the title). What's already in a feed when you add it isn't announced. Look for an RSS or feed link on the website, or try its address with /feed or /rss on the end. Up to 50 feeds.

Setup-mode rescue · after the Dot restarts

After a restart an unregistered Echo goes into setup mode (spinning orange ring), switches your hotspot off and broadcasts an open network of its own. With its name below, Harald hops onto it for a moment, checks it's your Dot, switches your hotspot back on and hops home. Look for the name in your phone's Wi-Fi list while the ring is orange (like "Amazon-XY7"). If nothing answers there, the tries get further apart (up to 30 minutes); unplugging the Dot for 10 seconds starts it afresh.

Find your dragon by Wi-Fi (no GPS): off

For a dragon with no GPS. Harald sends the list of Wi-Fi networks in range to beaconDB, a free community service that knows roughly where routers are, and it answers with roughly where your dragon is.

Less private: the networks around you are where you are. Every lookup tells beaconDB where your dragon is, to within tens of meters, and its server logs keep your internet address and the time for 28 days. It only runs while there's no GPS and Harald is on your hotspot, at most every two minutes, and not while the same networks are around.

A Wi-Fi position claims lands and realms, spots landmarks and discovers green spaces. It never counts towards walk distances, walks, runs or rides, and nothing found this way is uploaded to WiGLE.

Offline map: off

The map page's streets without a signal. Once a day, when Harald has the internet, the pieces of the map covering the lands claimed since last time (and a little round them) are fetched and kept on the Pi. The map page draws them underneath OpenStreetMap's own, so wherever those can't load, these show through. They come from Protomaps, which publishes all of OpenStreetMap as one file each day for exactly this (OpenStreetMap's own servers don't allow it).

Privacy: which pieces are asked for shows Protomaps roughly where your dragon has been, to the nearest few hundred meters, as any online map does while you look at it. Forget my map deletes the offline map too.

Exclude home zone (extra privacy)
A circle around home where wardriving sightings (each network with where you heard it) are never recorded, so they're never uploaded to WiGLE. Without it, your sightings start and end at your door. The circle's middle is moved a random distance from home, so nobody can find home by finding the middle of the empty patch. Your home's own position is never stored. Networks inside still count as treats. Switch it on at home, with the GPS working: the first time, the circle goes around wherever Harald is.
