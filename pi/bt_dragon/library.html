How the e-reader works

The reader opens DRM-free EPUBs: many shops lock their e-books to their own apps, but books from places like Project Gutenberg and Standard Ebooks, and many independent publishers, are DRM-free. Tap or swipe at the sides to turn pages; A− and A+ change the text size; the list button opens the contents.

Pages. The reader splits each book into pages of about 250 words, so its page count won't match a printed copy. A book started from the shelf uses these pages.

Automatic tracking. While you read, the reader notes which pages you actually spend time on. A page counts once it has been on screen long enough to read it: about 10 to 20 seconds for a screenful of text, depending on how much fits on your screen. Flicking past pages doesn't count, and neither does time when the page is in the background. Each page only counts once, however often you reread it. The contents, cover, copyright page, index, glossary, notes, bibliography and similar parts aren't counted.

Nothing is logged by itself. When you close the reader, you'll see how many new pages you read, and can log them (they bring your dragon joy, like any reading) or log your reading yourself instead. If you've read nearly all of a book (90% or more), including its last page, logging finishes it.

Marking pages and chapters as read. Already read some of a book somewhere else? Open the contents and tick the chapters you've read, or tick Marked as read at the top of a page. With automatic tracking on, that box ticks itself the moment a page has been on screen long enough. Pages you mark yourself only count once the whole book is further on in the reader than the book's progress here. Pages that have counted never count twice, and unticking never takes progress away.

With automatic tracking off, the reader still remembers your place and you can still mark pages as read, but it doesn't count pages for you or show a summary. This setting is kept on each phone or computer.
